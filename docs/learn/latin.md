# Latein (course `la`)

## Scope and level
First year of school Latin for German speakers (Lehrgang topics of Prima, Felix, Cursus), classical Latin. The student
reads: translates into German, types forms (declension and conjugation) and sees German grammar terms. No Latin voice,
no free production (`produce: no`). Conventions: nouns as "puella, -ae f.", verbs in the 3rd person ("amat, amant"),
long vowels are not written (macrons appear nowhere in the data).

## Units
1. Die a-Deklination: Nom. Sg./Pl., maskuline a-Stämme (agricola, nauta), 3rd person present (-t/-nt). Forms: puellae, feminae, agricolae, nautae.
2. Der Akkusativ: -am/-as, Subjekt und Objekt, non. Forms: puellam, puellas, rosam, vias.
3. Die o-Deklination und esse: -us/-um, neuter Nom.=Akk., -a in the plural; sum, es, sumus, estis (est, sunt are words).
4. Präpositionen: ad, per (Akk.); cum, sine, a/ab, e/ex (Abl.); in with Abl./Akk.; Abl. endings -a/-o/-is.
5. Adjektive der a-/o-Deklination: magnus, -a, -um; KNG-Kongruenz; forms magna, magnum, boni, novae, parva.
6. Genitiv und Dativ: -ae/-arum, -i/-orum; Dativ -ae/-o/-is; dare and narrare with Dativ and Akkusativ; full paradigm overview in the tips.
7. a- und e-Konjugation: personal endings, ego/tu/nos/vos; forms of amare and habere (1st and 2nd person, both numbers).
8. i-Konjugation und Imperativ: audio...audiunt, imperative singular/plural, question words, salve/vale.

Totals: 88 words, 63 sentences, 43 forms, 22 fills, 15 facts (231 items).

## Design decisions
- The Imperfekt and the 3. Deklination are not included. Real Lehrgänge teach them after the present tense and the perfect, so a first-year outlook would be too thin; unit 8 is the i-Konjugation with the imperative instead. A second volume can add them.
- All Latin forms in sentences are covered by words (the verb entry lists both 3rd person forms), form answers or `known:` lines.
- Meanings of verbs are infinitives with the 3rd person as alternative, because typed German is accepted forgivingly.
- Tests recompute every declension and conjugation answer from endings typed again in the test file.

## What a teacher should double-check
- Whether "laut rufen" is the right second meaning of clamare and "betrachten" for spectare in the textbook the class uses.
- "oppidum" as "Stadt" (strictly a provincial town), "templum" as "Tempel", "villa" as "Landhaus".
- That "Marcus" and "Iulia" as given names without explanation suit the class.
- Unit 7 tip says the a before the endings is long; macrons are deliberately not written elsewhere.
- Sentences are original; check that none uses a word the class book teaches later (the coverage check only covers this course's own order).
- The Vokativ is only used implicitly (Venite, amici!), not taught.
